# Frozen Stage 2 research protocol

## Research question

**RQ:** To what extent can structural nonlinearities explain the experimentally observed frequency-doubled response?

The observation of bending–torsion coupling and frequency-doubled/second-harmonic response is not itself claimed as novel. The research target is why and how a 2f component is generated or amplified, and how much can reasonably be attributed to structural nonlinearities after validation.

## Hypotheses and controls

- **H1:** For a linear time-invariant structural model under an input containing only a single harmonic frequency, no significant second harmonic should be generated by the structure itself.
- **H2:** Certain asymmetric/quadratic/piecewise structural nonlinearities may generate or amplify a second-harmonic response.
- **H3:** The presence of 2f alone does **not** identify cable slackening or any unique physical mechanism.
- **H4:** If disabling one nonlinear mechanism substantially reduces `A_2f/A_f` under otherwise identical conditions, that mechanism is considered an important numerical contributor, not automatically a proven physical cause.
- **H5:** If experimentally observed 2f cannot be reproduced by reasonable structural nonlinear models after validation, structural nonlinearity alone will be considered insufficient and aeroelastic feedback will be identified as a possible missing mechanism.
- **H6:** Every comparison must distinguish forcing-generated 2f from structure-generated 2f.

The negative control is a linear model under single-frequency forcing with no input 2f. The positive software control injects a known, small 2f term into the input to verify detection only; it is not mechanism evidence.

## Meaning of `f`

The symbol `f` must never be used without a recorded reference. Every analysis metadata record must contain `frequency_reference`, with exactly one of:

- `FORCING`: the externally imposed excitation frequency;
- `MODE`: a structural natural/modal frequency;
- `RESPONSE_PEAK`: the dominant measured or computed response peak;
- `EXPERIMENT_DEFINED`: a frequency defined by the source experiment and retained with its provenance.

The Stage 2 controlled ROM uses `frequency_reference: FORCING`. A change of reference requires a new traceable analysis record; frequencies with different references must not be silently compared as if equivalent.

## Candidate mechanisms

The controlled numerical design keeps the following distinct:

1. quadratic/asymmetric nonlinear coupling;
2. symmetric cubic stiffness;
3. nonlinear bending–torsion coupling;
4. cable-tension evolution represented by a documented surrogate;
5. tension-only/piecewise stiffness behavior represented by a surrogate; and
6. possible 1:2 modal interaction, to be tested only after modal properties are validated.

Symmetric cubic nonlinearity is not assumed to generate a strong 2f response. Cable slackening is not assumed. Aeroelastic feedback is not excluded, but it is outside the present structural-only screening scope.

## Hard scope boundaries

- no new wind-tunnel campaign;
- no field monitoring;
- no full CFD;
- no two-way CFD–FSI at this stage;
- no forced parameter fitting to manufacture a desired 2f peak;
- no cable-slackening claim without independent evidence;
- no large sweep, Monte Carlo study, optimization, or full experimental reproduction;
- no current job longer than approximately five minutes.

Current outputs are classified `STAGE2_MECHANISM_DEVELOPMENT` or `GENERIC_FE_PROTOTYPE`, never `FORMAL_PAPER_RESULT`. Formal inference requires real parameters, provenance, model verification, experimental validation, and permission-compatible use of the experimental records.
